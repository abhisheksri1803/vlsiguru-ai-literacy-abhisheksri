Artificial Intelligence (AI)
│
├── Conventional Rule-Based Programming
│   └── Explicitly coded rules (if-then logic)
│
├── Machine Learning (ML)
│   └── Learns patterns from data
│       └── Deep Learning (DL)
│           └── Generative AI (creates new content)
│               └── Large Language Models (LLMs)

->Five Distinctions
AI vs Rule-Based Programming:  
Rule-based systems follow fixed instructions; AI systems aim to mimic human-like decision-making.

AI vs ML:  
AI is the broad goal of “smart machines,” while ML is one method—machines learn from data instead of being explicitly programmed.

ML vs Deep Learning:  
ML can use simple algorithms (decision trees, regression), while DL uses multi-layered neural networks for complex tasks like vision or speech.

Generative AI vs Traditional AI:  
Generative AI doesn’t just classify or predict—it creates new outputs (text, images, music) based on learned patterns.

Automation vs AI:  
Automation executes predefined steps (like macros or scripts). AI adapts, learns, and makes decisions. Not all automation is AI, and not all AI involves ML (some AI is rule-based).


->Explicitly Programmed vs Learned from Data
Explicitly Programmed: Calculator operations, traffic light rules, fixed sorting algorithms.

Learned from Data: Spam filters, recommendation systems, speech recognition.
